Research philosophy
Ultimately, it is about how humanity proactively elevates the human condition through technology and education.

Technologies do not raise living standards on their own. People do, when education and tools let them see further and act with greater capability. My research follows that process step by step: how education and frontier technologies augment human potential, and how augmented people go on to understand the economy, create value and redesign the markets and institutions they live in.

Key research

Three ways people elevate their condition

A selection of key papers, organised by what augmented human potential makes possible. Each summary states what the study found and why it matters.

01

Designing new markets and institutions

Prosperity rests on markets and institutions that let strangers cooperate: money, lending, organisations and the rules that keep them stable. Blockchain lets people redesign these as code, while liquidity, policy and crowd behaviour test whether the designs hold. I study when they hold together, where they break, and how to make them worthy of trust.

Can code-based money, lending and organisations earn the trust that traditional institutions took centuries to build?

2026Journal of Financial Stability

Excess liquidity, cryptocurrency returns, and the moderating role of economic policy uncertainty

Across ten major cryptocurrencies from 2019 to 2024, periods of U.S. excess liquidity coincide with monthly returns about 12 percentage points higher. Rising policy uncertainty cuts that premium roughly in half, and in China it more than offsets it.

Why it matters: regulators can watch stablecoin issuance, leverage and funding conditions to anticipate liquidity-driven speculation.

2025Global Finance Journal

The determinants of funding liquidity risk in decentralized lending

Examines how liquidity is interconnected across DeFi lending platforms and identifies what drives their funding liquidity risk, the decentralised equivalent of a bank run.

Why it matters: points protocol designers to the levers, such as interest-rate design, that keep open lending markets stable.

2023Journal of Industrial and Business Economics

Are the stabilities of stablecoins connected?

Among the five largest stablecoins, instability in USDT and USDC significantly drives instability in the others, with weaker evidence for the reverse.

Why it matters: shows users and regulators which coins to watch to protect confidence in digital money.

2026North American Journal of Economics and Finance

Herding behavior of cryptocurrency during the 2024 U.S. presidential election

Examines whether cryptocurrency investors abandoned their own information and followed the crowd around a major political event, building on earlier evidence of herding during the Russia–Ukraine conflict.

Why it matters: shows how political events can turn independent investors into a herd, with consequences for market stability.

2026Working paper

Why do decentralized autonomous organizations recentralize?

Investigates why organisations designed to run without central control drift back toward concentrated decision-making.

Why it matters: informs how digital organisations can stay open to broad participation as they grow.

2023Report for the Ethereum Foundation

Monetary policy in the age of cryptocurrencies

Reviews the history of monetary technologies from commodity to fiat money, assesses the challenges and opportunities of adopting programmatic cryptocurrencies as legal tender, and compares proof-of-work and proof-of-stake issuance.

Why it matters: asks how new forms of money would affect growth, inflation and the concentration of wealth.

02

Creating new types of value

Frontier technologies and better measurement create forms of value that did not exist before: digitally scarce assets, signals extracted by AI from millions of posts, new measures of uncertainty, and AI agents able to transact. I study where this value comes from and how people can capture it.

What new value can people create when machines can read, measure, reason and transact?

2024International Review of Economics & Finance

Revisiting the determinants of cryptocurrency excess return: does scarcity matter?

Programmed scarcity is a major determinant of excess returns for proof-of-work cryptocurrencies.

Why it matters: gives investors an economic basis, rather than hype, for understanding digital-asset value.

2025China Finance Review International

Decoding market sentiment: the power of ChatGPT in explaining Bitcoin returns from X data

Sentiment indicators built with ChatGPT from Bitcoin-industry posts on X significantly explain Bitcoin returns, even after controlling for established sentiment measures.

Why it matters: everyday AI tools can help financial professionals detect signals that earlier indicators missed.

2024Journal of Risk and Financial Management

Blockchain: the economic and financial institution for autonomous AI?

AI agents can research and decide, but cannot act on their decisions because they lack access to contracts, financial services and marketplaces. Viewed as an institutional technology, blockchain can provide digital-native institutions that let them do so.

Why it matters: agents that can transact on our behalf could greatly augment human productivity.

2020Real Estate Economics

A new measure of real estate uncertainty shocks

Constructs an uncertainty measure specific to real estate. It explains about twice as much of the variation in housing prices and starts as a leading macroeconomic uncertainty measure, and it affects prices as well as construction.

Why it matters: better measurement of uncertainty helps households, developers and policymakers time major decisions.

03

Augmenting potential through education

Human potential is built in classrooms. Generative AI can now answer assignments and even grade them, so universities must rethink what students learn and how learning is assessed. I measure what AI can and cannot do, and design assessment that develops distinctly human strengths.

If machines can pass the test, what should education develop in people?

2023Australasian Journal of Educational Technology

Race with the machines: assessing the capability of generative AI in solving authentic assessments

A framework based on Bloom's taxonomy tests ChatGPT-4, ChatGPT-3.5, Bard and Bing on economics assessments. They excel at lower-order tasks but are weakest at creating, building theory-based arguments and referencing.

Why it matters: tells educators which higher-order skills to cultivate as AI handles the rest.

2025Journal of Applied Research in Higher Education

Authentic assessment designs and students' self-perception of leadership in business education in Vietnam

Team-based authentic assessment strengthens students' sense of their own leadership, especially when combined with extra-curricular activity. Female students report higher leadership confidence, contrary to conventional expectations.

Why it matters: assessment design can develop leadership, a skill AI cannot supply.

2024Working paper

Evaluating the efficacy of generative artificial intelligence in grading

Introduces a framework for testing whether tools such as ChatGPT-4 can grade complex take-home assessments in economics, weighing gains in efficiency and reduced bias against clear limitations.

Why it matters: frees educator time for feedback and mentoring while keeping judgement in human hands.

Beyond research

The personal side of human potential

The same conviction that guides my research guides my life outside it: people grow by cultivating the mind, staying curious and seeing the world first-hand.

01

Meditation

A daily practice of attention and calm. Clarity of mind is the inner foundation of human potential, and it shapes how I think, teach and decide.

02

Exploring

Curiosity about places, ideas and people. Exploring keeps me close to how economies and communities actually work on the ground.

03

Learning

A lifelong student, moving between German, Vietnamese and English, between economics and new technologies, and always looking for the next question.

04

Travelling

Often by motorbike, along coastlines and through mountains. Travel is where reflection and discovery meet.
